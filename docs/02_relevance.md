## Discussion protocol
Recruit 3–5 distinct representatives, ideally two document authors, one or two reviewers and one administrator or records officer. A substantive agreement with an actual client is an alternative allowed by the checklist, but it must define the workflow and requirements in enough detail. No participant identities or findings should be invented.

Explain the student project, request voluntary participation and permission for notes, and use participant codes instead of names in the repository. Do not collect confidential documents. Each discussion should last approximately 20–30 minutes and use an anonymised example supplied with permission or a synthetic example.

1. Describe the last internal document you prepared or reviewed and each step it followed.
2. How did you identify the latest version, reviewer and final decision?
3. Where did delays, duplicate versions or missing information arise? Ask for an example rather than suggesting an answer.
4. Which document types, metadata and deadline rules are essential?
5. Who may view, modify, approve and archive a document? What happens when someone is absent?
6. What happens after requested changes, an incorrect approval or an expired deadline?
7. Which existing tools do you use and what would make configuration of those tools sufficient?
8. Perform the prototype tasks. Which steps are unclear and what is missing?

## Evidence and requirement conversion
Record participant code, role, date, consent, process description, anonymised supporting example, observation or short quotation, confidence and candidate requirement IDs. Separate what the participant said from the team's interpretation. Compare accounts across roles and record contradictions. Promote a hypothesis to a confirmed requirement only after its evidence reference and acceptance criterion are recorded. A single person's preference does not establish organisation-wide policy.
