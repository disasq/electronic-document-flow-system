# Literature search and verification log — 24 October 2026

## Scope and provenance

This is a reproducible **targeted update** for the first pre-defence, run on 24 October 2026 (Asia/Almaty). It does not reconstruct the exact searches behind the earlier 19 and 23 October reading: those query strings, result lists and hit counts were not saved. It is not a systematic review, exhaustive search or PRISMA flow.

The questions were: (1) which organisational factors affect electronic document or records management adoption, (2) how process redesign and explicit workflow models inform approval routing, and (3) how authorization models constrain document access. The first two questions also guide comparison with the ten reported Astana interviews. The scientific review and source-by-source matrix are in [literature_review.md](literature_review.md); IDs resolve in the [bibliography](../12_references.md) and [source register](../../evidence/source_register.json).

## Inclusion, exclusion and inspection

The title screen covered 40 **query positions** (four first pages of ten; positions are not necessarily unique works). For inclusion in the scientific matrix, a work needed identifiable authors, a journal or conference venue, a DOI verified in Crossref, and a clear connection to EDMS/ERMS adoption, process redesign, workflow modelling, authorization or Kazakhstan context. We preferred 2022–2026 publications. S07 (1996) and S08 (1998) are explicit exceptions for foundational RBAC and workflow models. Product documentation and vendor claims can inform design comparisons but do not count as scientific publications.

We excluded preprints from the scientific count, duplicate DOI records, promotional pages, works unrelated to document or records management, and clinical electronic-health-record results that did not answer these project questions. Broad digital-transformation articles without a useful workflow or implementation finding were not added. Selection was by relevance and inspectable evidence; no formal risk-of-bias scoring or full-text appraisal of all retrieved records was done.

The first-page screen retained or reconfirmed four unique IDs: S05, S26, S31 and S32. S31 and S32 were added to the existing ten-publication matrix. S31's publisher abstract, methods and discussion sections were inspectable through the indexed article page. For S32 we inspected the indexed abstract only; its row therefore states a narrow, survey-level conclusion and does not claim observed system adoption. The other existing publications were retained because their direct DOI records and previously recorded inspected materials remained aligned with the review questions.

## DOI and citation verification

On 24 October 2026 the **Crossref Works API** `GET https://api.crossref.org/works/{DOI}` returned a journal-article metadata record for all twelve scientific entries: S01–S08, S26, S27, S31 and S32. We checked the title, author list, publication year, venue and DOI against the [bibliography](../12_references.md). Four existing titles (S01, S04, S05 and S06) had missing punctuation compared with Crossref; the bibliography and register were corrected. Crossref verifies bibliographic registration, not the validity of every claim within an article or the current publisher access status.

The resulting first pre-defence evidence set has **12 scientific publications**, of which **10 were published in 2022–2026**, plus **20 non-scientific official, technical or vendor sources** used elsewhere in the project. Findings and transfer limits for every scientific item are stated in the [publication matrix](literature_review.md#structured-analysis-of-scientific-publications).
