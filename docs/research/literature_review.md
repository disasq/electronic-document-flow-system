# Literature review and publication matrix

## Review questions and selection
This targeted scoping review asks which EDMS adoption issues matter, how workflow correctness should be represented, and how access control affects document processing. Exploratory searches on 19 and 23 October 2026 used combinations of electronic document management system, electronic records, workflow, business process management, digital transformation, authorization, access control and Kazakhstan. Exact queries and hit counts for those earlier searches were not retained, so they are not presented as reproducible searches. On 24 October 2026 we ran a documented update in OpenAlex, checked the scientific DOIs against Crossref and inspected the relevant publisher or indexed article pages. The exact queries, dates, limits, screening decisions and metadata checks are in the [search log](search_log_2026-10-24.md).

The scientific core contains twelve journal or conference publications. Ten date from 2022–2026 and fall within the preceding five years. Two foundational papers from 1996 and 1998 explain RBAC and explicit workflow reasoning. The [numbered bibliography](../12_references.md) and [source register](../../evidence/source_register.json) contain 32 sources in total. The other twenty are official product, technology, security or vendor documents used in the alternatives and design analysis; they are not counted as scientific publications.

Scientific selection required identifiable authors, a journal or conference venue, a verifiable DOI and a direct connection to adoption, process redesign, workflow modelling, access control or Kazakhstan's document-management context. Recent publications were preferred; the two older papers were retained solely for foundational models. We excluded preprints, promotional materials and unrelated records systems from the scientific count. We also screened out health-record studies when their clinical setting did not answer the departmental document-workflow questions. This is not an exhaustive database search or a PRISMA systematic review. Some articles were inspected only through abstracts or selected sections; the table names that limit, and its findings do not exceed the inspected evidence. Full-text appraisal would strengthen the final diploma submission.

## Critical synthesis
EDMS reviews [S01, S02], the newer ERMS synthesis [S31] and the adoption surveys [S26, S32] support treating adoption, ease of use, staff capability and governance as part of the problem. The surveys concern intention rather than observed use and report associations, not causal effects. The ERMS review covers recordkeeping more broadly than approval routing. Ten reported Astana conversations provide qualitative local examples of version, status and approval-history problems ([interview analysis](../02_relevance.md)); they do not establish how often these problems occur or the effect size of a new system. Accordingly, we retain usability and onboarding requirements and will test both perceived usability and task completion in a defined pilot workflow.

The Kazakhstan archive study [S27] gives local documentary context, but addresses state archives and is based on theoretical investigation. The ten interview notes provide more direct workflow examples, yet they span different sectors and do not establish one organisation's policy. Neither source justifies adding legally binding signatures or government integration to this project's first-stage scope.

BPM work [S03, S05, S06] supports analysing a process before automating it. Bibliometric activity is not evidence that our system improves performance. The project therefore selects concrete tasks and a baseline rather than claiming innovation because it uses a web interface. The custom-system hypothesis is a small, visible approval journey with ownership, a frozen review version and a recorded next action.

The access-control review and RBAC foundation [S04, S07] motivate a distinction between role membership and document relationships. A reviewer role does not grant permission to decide every document. Ownership and reviewer assignment must be checked for read, edit and decision operations. The architecture uses one policy service and negative tests for object-level access.

Explicit workflow logic [S08] informs a transition table and tests for forbidden paths. This project uses a finite-state model and does not claim formal Petri-net verification. Its key invariants are that a decision references a fixed version, only the assigned reviewer can decide, and archived documents cannot be edited.

The resulting contribution is an engineering design and evaluation for a bounded departmental setting. The publications and interview notes do not prove that a new product is commercially superior to established systems. That decision remains conditional on pilot-specific rules, measured evaluation and a build-versus-configure checkpoint.
